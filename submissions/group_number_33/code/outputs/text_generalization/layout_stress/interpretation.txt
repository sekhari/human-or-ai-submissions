Post hoc layout-heldout diagnostic, frozen before its three model fits.

Validation is the deterministically selected largest eligible layout group:
multiline without blank paragraphs, 2,826 rows (2,600 human / 226 AI).
Training is the complementary 5,174 rows (1,400 human / 3,774 AI).
Other groups: no newlines 2,479 (1,380 human / 1,099 AI); blank paragraphs
2,695 (20 human / 2,675 AI). The latter is ineligible for validation because
it contains fewer than 200 human examples.

Fixed-model AUROC:
style_hgb31: 0.798772975 (fit 7.080 seconds)
raw_punct_word12_lr_C4: 0.499012934 (fit 1.311 seconds)
char_min_36_lr8: 0.507593601 (fit 8.828 seconds)

Interpretation: all three models lose substantial discrimination under this
layout-conditioned distribution change. Style HGB retains materially more
ranking information than either fixed lexical model; the two lexical models
are near chance here. These results do not support assuming that the lexical
models are automatically more robust to formatting/source shift. The strong
association between layout group and label makes ordinary random/topic
results insufficient to establish transfer to another collection mixture.

AUROC is not directly a function of label prevalence, but the protocol also
changes the class-conditional source/text mixture and the training imbalance.
This is deliberately a difficult post hoc stress diagnostic, not an unbiased
estimate of unknown test performance. No tuning or model choice occurred
within this diagnostic; exactly the three requested configurations were run.

Verified: group assignments, source/development positions, disjoint partition,
validation IDs and labels, finite raw/z scores, training-only z-score formula,
and all three recomputed AUROCs. No outer or test data was used.
