% ---------------- Q1.1 ----------------
We compared four representations. \textbf{(1) Sparse $n$-grams:} scikit-learn's
default tokenizer with lowercasing and no stemming or stopword removal, since
function-word frequency proved informative. Word 1--2-grams
(\texttt{min\_df}=2, $\le$200k features) capture phrasing such as ``overall'' or
``such as''; character 3--5-grams (\texttt{char\_wb}, \texttt{min\_df}=3,
$\le$300k features) capture sub-word patterns. Both use sublinear TF-IDF.
\textbf{(2) Stylometric features:} 26 statistics (word/sentence length,
type--token ratio, function-word, punctuation and newline rates).
\textbf{(3) Fine-tuned transformers} (DistilRoBERTa and RoBERTa-base) with their
byte-level BPE tokenizer, truncated to 256 tokens. \textbf{(4) Perplexity
features:} statistics of per-token log-probability, entropy and rank under GPT-2
(13 features) and under GPT-2 + GPT-2 Medium together, including Binoculars
scores (25 features). After finding that blank lines leaked the label (0\% of
human vs.\ 66\% of AI training passages) and did not transfer to the test set,
we \textbf{collapsed all whitespace} before the transformer and perplexity
models, so they cannot use formatting.

Every setting was trained on the same 8,000-row training split and scored on the
fixed, stratified 2,000-row validation split (seed 42); Kaggle scores were never
used for tuning. For logistic regression, validation AUROC rose from 0.932
($C=0.25$) to a plateau of 0.949--0.951 for $C \ge 2$; we kept $C=4$, since
$C=8$ was only 0.0006 higher, which is within noise. The GBM grid ranged from
0.986 to 0.995; we kept the default (0.9945), because the best setting (0.9953)
differed by under 0.001 and used twice as many trees. For the transformers, the
second epoch helped both models (DistilRoBERTa 0.9821$\to$0.9867, RoBERTa-base
0.9863$\to$0.9926). Ensemble weights were taken from a flat region: the top 10
combinations all scored 0.9959--0.9960, and equal weights reached 0.9953, so
the choice is not fragile.

Our final submission is a \textbf{rank-average ensemble of five models}:
fine-tuned RoBERTa-base (weight 0.5) and DistilRoBERTa (0.1), two perplexity
GBMs (GPT-2, 0.2; GPT-2 + GPT-2 Medium with Binoculars, 0.1) and word+char
TF-IDF logistic regression (0.1). It reaches 0.9960 validation AUROC and
\textbf{[X.XXX] on the public leaderboard}. Validation score alone was not
enough: the stylometric GBM had a top validation AUROC (0.9945), but its strength
came from a blank-line artifact, and it scored only 0.858 on Kaggle, the largest
validation-to-test drop of any model ($-0.137$). We therefore favored models that
see no formatting and whose errors complement each other. On Kaggle, the
transformer alone (DistilRoBERTa, 0.894) beat every classical model by 3 points;
adding TF-IDF raised it to 0.902 and adding GPT-2 perplexity features to 0.921,
and RoBERTa-base was the strongest single model on validation. The full pipeline
trains in about an hour on a laptop GPU, and its weights are stable ($\pm$0.0001
across the top 10), suggesting it is not tuned to validation noise.
